### Article 1: How to choose an attar supplier in Chennai

Most wholesale buyers start with a price list. A better first step is a one-page brief: the sales channel, the customers you serve, the price band, the scent families, the pack sizes and the date the stock must be on the shelf.

Once the brief is clear, compare suppliers on five points. First, can they supply the exact pack, not only a similar one. Second, can they explain the difference between a natural oil, a reconstituted accord and a fragrance compound. Third, do they show the actual bottle, cap and label rather than a studio image. Fourth, can they quote the landed cost per finished unit. Fifth, do they answer questions before the order as quickly as they promise to answer after it.

Ask for a sample pack and ship it to yourself. The parcel tests the cap seal, the fill level and the label before the bulk order does. Check the opening, the middle and the dry-down, and keep a retained sample so a later batch can be compared.

At Hallmark of Excellence, loose oils start at 100g and branded Arees Perfumes packs start at 60 pieces across a combined order. That lets a retailer test several scents without overstocking one. The point is not the lowest quote. The point is a range you can reorder with confidence.

### Article 2: Roll-on attar MOQs: what retailers should ask

A 60-piece minimum sounds simple until the first order arrives with one scent that sells and five that sit on the shelf. Ask how a combined order works, and whether the 60 pieces can be split across scents.

Then ask about the pack itself. Is the fill consistent? Does the ball roll without flooding? Is the cap tight enough for a handbag? Does the label sit straight, and will the adhesive survive Chennai humidity? These details decide whether the pack feels like a retail product or a sample.

Calculate the landed cost per unit, not the price per bottle. Add the cap, label, individual wrapping, carton and freight. A cheaper bottle that leaks or a label that lifts will cost more in returns than the saving on the unit price.

For a first order, choose a narrow range: two or three proven scent families, one or two distinctive oils and a pack size that matches the shelf. Reorder the winners and add new scents from the reorder, not from the first catalogue.

Arees Perfumes supplies 6ml and 8ml roll-on attars from 60 pieces across a combined branded order, with mixed-scent packs so a counter can offer variety without overstocking. Ask for the exact mix and unit prices in writing.

Read the full 2027 checklist: https://www.areesperfumes.in/guides/wholesale-attar-sourcing-guide-2027

### Article 3: Bakhoor packaging and humidity: a checklist

Bakhoor is often packed in cartons that travel through heat and humidity before they reach the shelf. The fragrance may be stable, but the packaging can still fail.

Check four things before a bulk order. First, the individual wrapping: is each piece protected from the next? Second, the carton: are there dividers, and is the board strong enough for stacking? Third, the label: does the adhesive hold in warm conditions? Fourth, the storage instruction: does the retailer know how to keep the product dry after delivery?

Ask the supplier for a short video of the actual packing, not only a product photo. If you sell online, send one sample parcel to yourself before the bulk order. The parcel is the real test.

For export, confirm the documents and the destination labelling rules before dispatch. The buyer remains responsible for import requirements, so check them early rather than at the port.

Arees Perfumes supplies bakhoor and traditional incense for specialty stores, gifting and export orders. Share the destination, quantity and pack format so the team can confirm availability, packing and lead time.

Read the full 2027 checklist: https://www.areesperfumes.in/guides/wholesale-attar-sourcing-guide-2027

### Article 4: Attar vs perfume oil: what retailers should stock

The label matters less than the customer. A traditional attar buyer often wants an alcohol-free oil with a heritage feel and accepts natural variation. A perfume-oil buyer may want a familiar scent profile, consistent packaging and a wider range.

Stock both only when you can explain the difference at the counter. Train the staff on three questions: Is it alcohol-free? Is it a natural oil, a blend or a compound? How long does it last on skin? A clear answer builds trust and reduces returns.

For a gift shop, start with roll-on packs in familiar floral, musk and oud families. For a perfume counter, add 12ml oils and a few distinctive blends. For a blender or private-label brand, loose oils from 100g are the relevant format.

Do not compare a 100g loose oil price with a 6ml retail pack price. Compare the cost per finished unit and the margin after packaging. Then test the dry-down on skin, not only the opening.
